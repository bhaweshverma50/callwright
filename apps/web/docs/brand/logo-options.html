Four directions for the Callwright mark

A wright is a maker: shipwright, playwright. Callwright makes voice agents, so each direction draws on one of three ideas: turn-taking between two voices, the signal chain a call runs through, and the craft of making. Every symbol is built from circles, arcs and straight lines on a 48 unit grid, then tested at 16px, in the sidebar, the collapsed rail and the home page, light and dark.

A

Handoff

A C cut at the waist, its lower half stepped forward.

The top half is the caller, the bottom half the agent. One speaks, the other picks up the turn a beat later. Turn-taking is the hardest thing a voice agent does, and it is the moment Callwright times and scores.

The wordmark carries the same cut in its C, so the name and the mark are one idea.

Motion
On hover the halves slide level and step apart again: a turn changing hands.
Watch out
The most conventional of the four. The step is what makes it ours; without it, it is a plain C.

Dovetail

Two blocks locked by a dovetail joint.

A dovetail is the joint a wright cuts so two pieces hold without glue. Tails and pins alternate like turns in a call, and the seam between them is a trapezoid wave: a signal.

The W in the wordmark is cut on the same profile, flat valleys and a flat-topped tail, set in Unbounded capitals like a maker's stamp.

Motion
On load the lower block slides up into the tails and locks.
Watch out
Abstract: it needs the name beside it until people learn it, and the seam closes up below 16px.

C

Thread

One stroke, never lifted, writes c and w.

A call is one continuous path from the caller through the chain and back. The last upstroke rises above the rest: the agent answering.

The wordmark keeps Geist's plain letters and swaps in the same w, so the lift shows wherever the name does.

Motion
The stroke draws itself in once, end to end, then rests.
Watch out
A monogram, so it says cw rather than a picture. Gradient strokes need care on light surfaces.

Two voices

W is a double v: one v per voice.

The caller is cobalt, the agent violet. Where they cross the overlap turns heat pink: both talking at once, which a good agent handles and Callwright shows you. In one colour the overlap is cut out.

The wordmark uses the same crossed w in Unbounded, the app's display face.

Motion
The two v's slide in from either side and the overlap lights as they meet.
Watch out
The crossed w in the lowercase wordmark is subtle under 14px; the symbol carries it.
